How the algorithm works
  1. Use existing native-frame Sapiens2-5B poses and preserve each clip's source-frame mapping.
  2. Align visible foot landmarks to a source reference using translation and rotation. This removes a common reference-frame motion under a fixed-feet assumption; it does not identify camera intrinsics.
  3. Map named joints to 17 Human3.6M joints, derive the pelvis/spine/head approximations, and apply the official H36M-fine-tuned MotionBERT model with flip averaging.
  4. Run OpenSim 4.6 inverse kinematics on a custom pelvis, legs and trunk model. Segment proportions follow the estimated poses. One model metre represents nominal leg length, not measured stature.
  5. Compute knee flexion, range, bilateral difference, timing, trunk coordination and frontal-plane change. Apply quality gates before form rules.
  6. Produce an after-repetition screening result and specific review prompts. Centered processing uses future frames within the recording; it is not a validated prospective warning system.

OpenSim residuals measure agreement with estimated markers, not anatomical accuracy. The model has no separate lumbar or ankle/foot mechanism and cannot diagnose spinal rounding, heel pressure, or joint loading. ARED generalization is unvalidated.
