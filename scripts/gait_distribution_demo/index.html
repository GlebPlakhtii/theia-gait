A new unsupervised model learns motion from people together. Amputation status is never an input, training target, or threshold. It identifies unusual movement by body region.

Unusual means different from this recorded population. It does not establish incorrect technique, injury, or a need to adjust a prosthesis.

02 / HOW THE MODEL WAS TRAINED

A pooled movement distribution.

Learn patterns, without group labels

Two-component Gaussian mixtures model seven body regions separately for frontal/rear and side views. Four angular motion features come directly from cached Sapiens2 poses. The prior experiment’s good/bad targets and healthy-only reference bands are not used.

Hold people out

Five fixed splits give each identified person one test fold. Training, calibration and test people are disjoint. Each person receives equal reference weight, then each trial and clip. Camera views remain separate.

Localize the observation

Scores highlight a body region, not a diseased muscle. The OpenSim anatomy is generic; arms are schematic extensions because Gait2392 has no arm bones. Muscle colors localize the regional score and do not estimate muscle activation.

Coverage and observed review burden

Regional thresholds are empirical ranks from only 3–4 calibration people per fitted model. They do not guarantee a 5% error rate, especially when reviewing several regions. No clinician labels are available to measure clinical accuracy.

Scope and source references

This version supports overground walking in frontal/rear or side views. Running, treadmill footage, oblique/unknown views and other activities need their own supported reference distributions. Features are projected 2D angles: camera roll, perspective, assistance and pose errors may explain a deviation. One-second windows summarize local motion and are not full gait-cycle measurements.
